A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts mean different things. Measure the food, not the marketing — a practical capacity checklist for the three air fryers in the shop.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Updated 2026-11-20 · 4 min read · Sources

“6 quart,” “8 quart,” and “26 quart” sound like a simple ladder. They are not. A dual-basket 6 Qt is two 3-Qt spaces. An 8 Qt is one space. A 26QT oven is a rack cavity measured differently again. Use this checklist to compare capacity by the food you cook, not by the biggest number.

The checklist

#CheckHow to do it
1Measure your most-cooked foodPut your usual tray, bird, or batch of fries on a ruler. Write down length × width × height.
2Compare usable space, not quartsTwo 3-Qt baskets ≠ one 6-Qt basket. A 26QT oven fits a 12-inch pizza per listing — does that match your pans?
3Check basket countDual basket (Chefman 6 Qt) = cook main + side together. Single basket (Chefman 8 Qt) = one larger batch.
4Check depth, not just widthEmeril 26QT is 16.9 in deep. Measure counter depth + cabinet clearance before you buy.
5Check weight & storageEmeril 26.5 lb vs. Chefman ~13 lb. Heavier ovens tend to stay in one spot — is that your plan?
6Ignore “bought in past week”Popularity badges change daily and are not on this site. Capacity is a measurement, not a vote.

Frequently asked questions

Is 8 quarts always bigger than 6 quarts?

For usable single-batch space, yes — the 8 Qt is one basket. But the 6 Qt dual gives you two independent spaces, which can be more useful for a main + side meal. “Bigger” depends on the meal.

How do I know if a 12-inch pizza fits?

The Emeril listing says it fits a 12-inch pizza. Measure your actual pan (including handles) and compare it to the cavity dimensions on Amazon before you buy.
