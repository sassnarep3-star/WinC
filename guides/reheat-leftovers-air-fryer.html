Reheating holiday leftovers in an air fryer: the food-safety rules that matter

Two hours, four days, 165°F: the USDA numbers that decide whether tomorrow's plate is worth reheating — and where an air fryer fits, and where it does not.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20 · 4 min read · This category · Sources

The long weekend has a second act: the plates you reheat on Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Reheating is where a countertop air fryer earns its counter space — small portions, crisp surfaces, no big oven to pre-heat — but the safety rules do not change with the appliance. They come from USDA FSIS, and there are four numbers to know.

The four numbers from USDA FSIS

RuleNumberWhat FSIS saysSource
Time on the counter2 hours“Within 2 hours of cooking food or after it is removed from an appliance keeping it warm, leftovers must be refrigerated.” Left out longer than 2 hours at room temperature — or 1 hour when the room is over 90°F — goes in the trash.USDA FSIS, Leftovers and Food Safety (S27)
Fridge life3 to 4 days“Leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days or frozen for 3 to 4 months.”USDA FSIS, Leftovers and Food Safety (S27)
Reheat target165°F“When reheating leftovers, be sure they reach 165° F as measured with a food thermometer.”USDA FSIS, Leftovers and Food Safety (S27)
The danger zone40°F–140°F“Bacteria grow rapidly between the temperatures of 40° F and 140° F” — which is why the two-hour rule and the 165°F target exist.USDA FSIS, Leftovers and Food Safety (S27)

Where an air fryer fits — and where it does not

  • Fits: small, solid portions. Slices of roast, single servings of fried sides, a portion of casserole cut and spread in a layer. A convection appliance moves hot air fast, so thin uncovered pieces reheat quickly — but speed is not the safety question. The thermometer is. USDA’s target is 165°F measured in the food, whatever the appliance.
  • Fits with a caveat: moisture. FSIS advises covering leftovers while reheating because it “retains moisture and ensures that food will heat all the way through.” Moving hot air does the opposite to uncovered surfaces: expect drier edges and shorter times. A foil tent over the portion (kept off the heating element) is the compromise many cooks land on. Judge doneness by the thermometer, not the clock.
  • Does not fit: liquids. Sauces, soups and gravies have no place in an air-fryer basket. FSIS is specific about these: “Reheat sauces, soups and gravies by bringing them to a rolling boil” — a stovetop job.
  • Does not fit: big dense pieces. A half-bird or a thick roast reheats unevenly in any small convection cavity. FSIS notes dense food takes longer to heat through; carve into portions first, which also cuts the reheating time.

The thermometer part of the job

The 165°F check needs an instant-read you trust. FSIS’s thermometer guidance covers where to measure and how to keep the device honest — see FSIS — Food Thermometers. If your probe’s accuracy is a listing claim rather than a certification, that matters more at reheat time than any preset: the two thermometers in the shop publish their accuracy claims on their listings, and the verification record (verification.html) shows which cells could be confirmed and which could not.

Frequently asked questions

How long can Thanksgiving leftovers stay in the fridge?

USDA FSIS: “Leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days or frozen for 3 to 4 months.” Plan the last reheated plate for the fourth day, and freeze what will not make it.

What temperature should reheated leftovers reach?

165°F as measured with a food thermometer, per FSIS. Check the portion itself, not the basket — and for microwaved food, several places, because microwaves heat unevenly.

Can I reheat soup or gravy in an air fryer?

No — liquids belong on a hob. FSIS: “Reheat sauces, soups and gravies by bringing them to a rolling boil.” The air fryer’s job is the solid parts of the plate.

How fast do I have to refrigerate after dinner?

Within 2 hours of cooking (1 hour if the room is over 90°F), per FSIS. Divide big pots into shallow containers so the fridge can pull the temperature down fast; carve the bird before it goes in.
