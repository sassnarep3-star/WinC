Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Two shapes, two failure modes. The trade-offs that decide it are on the listing, if you know which fields to read.

Crisp Report desk·Published 20 November 2026 ·Reviewed 20 November 2026 ·4 min

Written as a comparison method. No unit on this desk has been cooked in, and no product below is recommended - the five links we were given are still unresolved, so this page deliberately names nothing.

The choice between a basket drawer and an oven-style unit is not about which cooks better. It is about which inconvenience you would rather live with: shaking and refilling a drawer in batches, or dedicating a permanent slice of counter to a box with a door. Both are workable. Both get returned for the wrong reason.

Start with the shape of what you actually cook

Write down three things you would realistically make in it twice a month. Not the aspirational list - the ordinary one. Then look at each item's geometry: a tray of wings lies flat, a whole small chicken does not; frozen fries want a wide, shallow layer, roasted chickpeas want air on all sides and tolerate a deeper pile.

A single-layer basket is the honest answer for anything you want crisped on two faces, because nothing sits on top of it. An oven-style unit with two racks and a rotisserie is the honest answer for volume in one go, because it trades contact with the surface for a second level. If your three items all lie flat, the second set of features is money spent on a mode you will not use.

Before you compare any two models, answer these three:

  • Do I need to cook one big flat layer or two stacked smaller ones?
  • Will food need turning halfway - and do I have a reason to care (yes for crisped surfaces, no for a casserole dish)?
  • Does anything I cook need a rotating spit, or a vertical clearance taller than the drawer can give? Those two requirements eliminate most drawers outright, whatever the spec sheet claims.

Six fields on the listing that actually decide it

FieldWhy it decides the shapeHow to check it
Internal chamber dimensions, not stated litresA volume figure can include the door cavity, the element housing or the rack stackCompare the length × width × height numbers against one item from your list. If only a litre figure is given, treat the capacity as unverified.
Number of rack or shelf positionsThis is the real difference between a drawer and an ovenCount the physical positions in the gallery, not the modes in the marketing copy.
Window and lightOven styles earn their door; drawer styles rarely need oneIf the listing has no window image, assume you will open it to look, which costs heat.
Removable parts listDrawer = one basket to wash; oven = racks, tray, door glassFind the parts diagram. Whatever is not shown as removable is what you will be wiping with a cloth.
Power figureCompare like with like between listings, not against a household guessNote the wattage each listing states and put the two numbers side by side; a bigger chamber at the same draw behaves differently from a small one.
Clearance stated in the manualOven styles usually want more breathing room around the exhaustThe manual is a linked PDF on most listings. Measure the space you have against that figure before the box arrives.
Rotation of the fan housingDetermines whether you can push it near a wallPhotograph the spot you have in mind and note what is beside the exhaust. If the answer is a cabinet side, a wall or a socket, the manual’s clearance figure becomes the deciding number, not the marketing copy.

Every row is a field you can verify on a listing in about a minute. None of them requires us to have tested a product.

The failure mode nobody mentions

Drawer units fail in households for a boring reason: the coating on the basket wears, and a replacement is either sold separately or not at all. Oven units fail for an equally boring reason: the door glass, the hinge and the seal are three more things that can be damaged by a scrubber, and they are rarely cheap or quick to replace.

Before you buy, run one search: the brand plus the word for the part you would most plausibly need in year two. If a coating or a gasket is not obtainable, the appliance has a shorter practical life than its warranty suggests, and the shape decision becomes a repairability decision. That is checkable from the listing page and the brand's own support area - not from a review score.

Decision line

Choose the drawer if you cook flat, single-layer food, wash as you go, and store the thing in a cupboard. Choose the oven style if you cook for more than two at once, want two racks or a rotisserie in the same run, and have a permanent stretch of counter that satisfies the clearance the manual asks for on the exhaust side. If both describe you, the tie-breaker is not performance - it is whether you would rather carry one basket to the sink or wipe a door.
