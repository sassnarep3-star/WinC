Cooking a turkey this week: where does the probe actually go?

USDA gives the number (165°F for all poultry) and the three places to check it. Which thermometer style gets you that reading — and where each one goes — decides the rest.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20 · 3 min read · This category · Sources

Thanksgiving has one number that matters more than any recipe: the internal temperature of the bird. The USDA FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart puts all poultry — breasts, whole bird, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing — at 165°F. The rest of the holiday thermometer question is mechanical: where the probe goes, and which style of thermometer can actually take that reading in a big, irregular bird.

The three places USDA names for whole poultry

The FSIS Food Thermometers factsheet is specific: "When cooking whole poultry, check the internal temperature in three places: the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing and the thickest part of the breast. Avoid the bone." The same factsheet adds a stuffing rule: for optimum safety, do not stuff whole poultry — and if you do, the center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F.

Which style of thermometer can take that reading?

FSIS's own types table splits the tools by whether they can stay in the food. Instant-read types (thermistor, thermocouple) are listed as "Not designed to remain in food while it is cooking" — you open the oven near the end and spot-check. Wireless/smart types are listed as "Designed to remain in the food while it is cooking" and "provide live status on internal temperature." The factsheet also carries the caution that decides this for every gadget drawer: "Do not leave your food thermometer in food while cooking unless the manufacturer's instructions specifically say it can withstand heat and has been designed for this purpose."

Two turkey-specific habits from the same factsheet

First: "When the food being cooked is large or irregularly shaped, such as with a beef roast, check the temperature in several places." A turkey is the definition of large and irregular — the three FSIS spots exist because a single reading in the breast can miss a cool pocket near the thigh. Second: "Color is not a reliable indicator that food is safe" — the factsheet's study section notes buyers who judged doneness by color and grill marks, and states the only way to know is a thermometer reading.
