Your first air fryer: the six checks before you buy

Buying your first air fryer? Six checks that come straight off the listing — capacity, footprint, weight, temperature range, power, dishwasher-safe parts — so you do not regret it in a month.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20 · 3 min read · This category · Sources

A first air fryer is a small purchase with a long afterlife: it lives on your counter, so the six things that decide whether you actually use it are the six things you check before you order. Every check below is a number or a word that appears on the product's own Amazon listing — no hands-on testing, no guessing.

The six checks

#CheckWhat to look for on the listingWhy it matters
1CapacityThe listed quarts and the basket split. The 6-quart dual-basket model lists two 3-quart baskets; the 8-quart lists one 8-quart basket; the 26-quart oven-style lists a 26QT (24.6 L) cavity.Capacity is a volume, not a serving count. Match it to the tray or bird you actually cook, not to the biggest number on the box.
2FootprintListed dimensions: 13.38×11.81×10.63 in (6 Qt), 12.75×13.5×10 in (8 Qt), 16.9×15.8×14.8 in (26 QT).Depth is where people get caught. The oven-style unit's listed depth is about 4 inches deeper than the 8-quart fryer's — measure your counter, not just the square footage you have free.
3WeightListed weights: 13.3 lb (6 Qt), 12.8 lb (8 Qt), 26.5 lb (26 QT).A unit you lift to clean or to move under a cabinet is a different purchase from one that stays put. The oven-style model is roughly double the weight.
4Temperature rangeThe 6-quart lists a 200–450°F digital range. The 8-quart's title says 450°F Hi-Fry while its own bullets say 200–400°F — the listing disagrees with itself, so read the page.The maximum heat decides what you can sear or bake. When a listing contradicts itself on its own page, that is exactly the number to confirm on the product page you land on.
5PowerThe 6-quart lists 120V / 1700W. The 8-quart lists 1000W / 120V.Wattage is a proxy for how fast the element heats. Two fryers in the same shop state different wattages — note your kitchen's circuit and the listed figure, not a round-up's number.
6Dishwasher-safe partsBoth Chefman listings describe nonstick, dishwasher-safe baskets; the oven-style unit lists racks, pan, drip tray and crisper basket as separate parts.Cleanup is the difference between a fryer you use weekly and one that sits. Count the parts that go in the dishwasher versus the parts you will hand-wash.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the biggest capacity to start?

No. Capacity is the first filter, but the right size is the one that matches the food you cook most. A 6-quart dual basket cooks two things at once; an 8-quart gives one larger batch; a 26-quart oven-style unit is built for trays and whole cooks. Check the listing's own dimensions before you check the quarts.

What if the listing says two different temperatures?

That happens. The 8-quart Chefman's title says 450°F Hi-Fry while the same page's bullets say 200–400°F. Read the product page you are actually sent to and confirm the range there; both readings are published on the Flagged for review page so nothing is hidden.

Is a higher wattage always better?

Not as a rule. The 6-quart lists 1700W and the 8-quart lists 1000W, yet they are different sizes doing different jobs. Wattage is one listed spec among six — weigh it with capacity, footprint and the parts you will wash.
