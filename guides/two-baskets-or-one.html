Two baskets or one? The air fryer shape decision in ten minutes

A dual-basket drawer, a single XL drawer and a French-door oven cavity are three different machines. Here is the one question that decides which shape you should be shopping, measured against the three listings in this shop.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20 · 3 min read · This category · Sources

Most air-fryer advice starts with capacity. Capacity is the wrong first question. The shape of the machine decides what you can cook at once, and the quart number only tells you how much of that shape you get.

There are three shapes in this shop, and they are not variations on one theme:

  • Two drawers — the Chefman 6 Quart, listed as two independent 3-quart baskets with a Sync Finish.
  • One large drawer — the Chefman 8 Quart, listed with a single XL basket and a size selector offering 6, 8 and 9 quarts under the same ASIN.
  • One cavity with doors — the Emeril 26QT French Door, listed as a countertop convection oven with racks and accessories rather than a basket.

The question that decides it

Ask what a typical weeknight meal in your kitchen looks like. Not a holiday, not a project cook — the thing you make when you are tired.

Notice what is not in that table: a ranking. The right shape is a function of your cooking, not of the machine. A dual-basket fryer is a worse purchase for someone who roasts whole chickens and a better one for someone who cooks salmon and broccoli on a Tuesday.

What each shape gives up

  • Two drawers give up flat space. Each basket is 3 quarts on the 6 Qt listing, so anything that only fits whole — a long rack, a wide tray — is out. You gain timing and lose footprint per item.
  • One big drawer gives up simultaneity. An 8-quart basket cooks one thing at one temperature. Two components become two sequential cooks or one compromise temperature.
  • The oven gives up the counter. The listed footprint is about 17 inches deep and the unit weighs 26.5 pounds, with doors that swing out. It is a permanent fixture, not a cupboard appliance, and it needs clearance in front and above.

Capacity numbers do not compare across shapes

Six quarts split into two baskets is not six quarts of usable flat space. Twenty-six quarts of cavity is not twenty-six quarts of basket. Comparing the numbers as if they were the same measurement is the single most common mistake in air-fryer shopping, and the listings do not help: the 6 Qt page even states its capacity in litres in one table and quarts in its title (FLG-018).

Compare shape against your cooking first, then compare the listed dimensions against the counter you actually have. The listed footprint — not the quart figure — is what decides whether the machine fits.

What to check on the page you land on

  • Which size is selected. The 8 Qt ASIN is a 6/8/9-quart family, so the price and reviews shown belong to the variation selected (FLG-023).
  • Whether a newer model is flagged. Amazon's own page states there is a newer model of the 8 Qt item (FLG-024).
  • The temperature ceiling. The 8 Qt title advertises 450°F while its bullets state 200–400°F (FLG-005). If your cooking depends on the top of the range, settle that on the page.

The short version

Two components, two drawers. One big batch, one big drawer. Trays and whole birds, an oven cavity. Everything else on the listing is detail — checkable detail, which is the only kind this site publishes.
