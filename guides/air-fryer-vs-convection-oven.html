Air fryer vs. convection oven: what actually changes?

Air fryer, convection oven, toaster oven — the names overlap. Here is what each listing in the shop actually says it does, and the one question that decides which shape you need.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20 · 3 min read · This category · Sources

The words air fryer, convection oven and toaster oven get used loosely, and the listings in this shop use them loosely too. The honest answer to "what actually changes" is: read what each listing says it is and does, because the marketing language overlaps more than the machine shapes do. Below is exactly what the three listings in the shop say — and the one question that decides between them.

So what actually changes between the shapes?

  • How food goes in. The two basket models load by pulling a basket out; the oven-style model opens French doors to a rack cavity. That is the clearest physical difference, and it is what the listings describe.
  • What fits. The oven-style listing states it can rotisserie a 6-lb chicken and bake a 12-inch pizza; the basket models are described around basket batches. That is the seller's own sizing claim, quoted as a claim.
  • The function language overlaps. The oven-style unit is the only one of the three that names toaster-oven modes such as toast and rotisserie in its listing. The basket models name fry, bake and dehydrate. If you specifically want toast or rotisserie, that is the listing that says it — read the page, because the same ASIN can show different name strings.

The one question that decides it

Ask yourself: do you want to cook in batches you can slide out, or do you want a small oven cavity for trays and whole cooks? If you cook two things at once and want the smallest footprint, the dual-basket shape is the one whose listing names that. If you want rack space for a tray, a pizza or a rotisserie, the oven-style listing is the one that describes it. Everything else — capacity, footprint, weight, temperature, power — is then a check on whichever shape you land on.

A note on the word "convection"

The oven-style listing names itself a "Countertop Convection Cooker"; the basket models are described as air fryers. Both move hot air, and this desk has not tested either, so it does not claim one technology beats the other. What it can do is point you to the listing that says the function you want — and that is the difference worth acting on.

Frequently asked questions

Is an air fryer the same as a convection oven?

Not as a machine, but the language overlaps. In this shop, the two basket models are listed as air fryers and the third is listed as a multi-function air fryer toaster oven / countertop convection cooker. Read what each listing says it does rather than assuming the names map one-to-one.

Which one can toast or rotisserie?

Of the three listings, the Emeril 26QT is the one whose listing names toaster-oven modes (toast, rotisserie) and states it can rotisserie a 6-lb chicken. The basket listings describe fry, bake and dehydrate. That is the seller's own copy, quoted as a claim.

Why do the same products use different names?

Amazon serves different product-name strings for the same detail page depending on the variant shown, so a name you saw elsewhere may differ from the page you land on. Confirm the function you want on the actual listing — the Flagged for review page records where the names have drifted.
