Trade-offs

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The two costs nobody prices in: what it takes to clean the thing, and the counter real estate it claims forever. Both are readable from the listing before you buy.

Air & Ember desk20 Nov 20268 min read

Appliances fail in kitchens for boring reasons: they don’t fit, they’re a pain to wash, or both. Here’s how to interrogate a listing for those two failure modes — using figures from our own five picks as worked examples. All dimensions and claims below were read from official listings on 20 Nov 2026, and manufacturer claims are labelled as such.

Counter space: the three measurements that matter

  • Footprint (D × W). The area it occupies when idle. Our air fryers: 13.38″ × 11.81″ (6 qt dual), 12.75″ × 13.5″ (8 qt), 16.9″ × 15.8″ (26 qt oven). The oven-style unit needs roughly the counter of a microwave — measure before you fall in love with the quarts.
  • Height + clearance. 10.63″, 10″, and 14.8″ respectively. If it lives under a cabinet, you also need clearance to tilt baskets up and open doors. The oven-style listing shows French doors — they need swing room the spec table won’t give you; the product video usually will.
  • Exit path. Can you pull the basket fully out with the unit against the wall? Basket depth (the part that slides) is longer than the housing suggests. If in doubt, leave 6–8 inches in front.

The wattage side-conversation

Wattage isn’t cleanup, but it’s the other spec people misread. Higher wattage generally means faster preheat and recovery, not “cooks better.” Observed: the 6 qt dual lists 1700 watts; the 8 qt lists 1000 watts (model RJ38-SQSS-8T per the listing). If your kitchen circuit is old and shared, that 1700 W draw is worth knowing before you plug in next to the microwave. (6 qt source ↗ · 8 qt source ↗)

The trade-off in one sentence: every basket, door, and accessory you add cooks something extra — and washes something extra. Buy the fewest parts that make the meals you actually cook.

A quick scoring method

  1. Write down the counter inches you can spare (measure, don’t estimate).
  2. List the parts you’d wash weekly on any given night.
  3. For each candidate, confirm both from the listing: footprint fits? parts washable the way you wash (dishwasher vs hand)?
  4. Any candidate failing either test is out — regardless of price.
