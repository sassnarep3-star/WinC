Interview

Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Every “10-in-1” is really two or three functions you’ll use daily and a shelf of ones you won’t. Interview the listing — and yourself — before the box arrives.

Air & Ember desk20 Nov 20267 min read

Multi-function machines are the easiest way to buy complexity you didn’t need. The fix isn’t avoiding them — our own register includes a 26 qt unit that air fries, toasts, bakes, and rotisseries — it’s asking better questions first. Here’s the interview, with worked answers from the official listing of our oven-style pick as an example.

The interview

  1. Which three functions will I use in week one? Write them down. If the answer is “air fry and reheat,” you’re paying for a brochure. The Emeril listing’s title advertises 24 one-touch presets while a bullet in the same page says 25 cooking pre-sets (source ↗) — either way, count how many of those presets match meals you actually cook.
  2. What does it replace — and what does it duplicate? If it replaces the toaster, where does the toaster go? If it duplicates the oven, ask why the counter space is worth it: the listing positions this unit for 4–6 people, a 12″ pizza, and a 6-lb chicken — that’s oven-sized work in an oven-sized footprint (16.9″ × 15.8″ × 14.8″ per its spec table).
  3. How many parts does a normal meal touch? Every function has accessories. The listing says six cooking accessories are included — good, but they live somewhere. Open your cupboards now and picture them.
  4. What’s the failure surface? Doors, gaskets, fans, non-stick coatings: more functions mean more things to service. Check what the listing states about warranty (our CHEF iQ listing states a two-year assurance; Chefman’s 8 qt bullet states a 1-year warranty) and keep the manual.
  5. Does a second tool solve it cheaper? Often the smarter buy is one single-function machine plus one precision tool. Our two thermometers are exactly that play: a $16.99 instant-read makes any oven — or any air fryer — more capable, without adding a single preset. (instant-read source ↗ · wireless source ↗)
The rule of three: name three functions you’ll use this month, one accessory you’ll store, and one failure you’d be annoyed by. If the listing answers all three cleanly, the machine is probably for you.
