FLG-001Needs review

What: All five supplied product codes are 9 characters long (B04G1MEwY, B02NbDxp3, B00hJ57DV, B0c8moW59, B0f3GKalj). Amazon’s own advertising documentation states “An ASIN is always 10 characters long, and either entirely numeric … or starts with a ‘B’.” The observed destinations (e.g. B0CDY277D3) are 10 characters — the supplied tokens are not.

Why it matters: The brief treats the supplied codes as checkable identifiers; as delivered, they can’t serve as ASINs. Intended-ASIN fields stay blank.

Evidence: Amazon Ads — targeting guide (ASIN length) ↗ · register with both code sets

Suggested action: Owner confirms intended product per code (S-08), or supply the real ASINs.

FLG-002Needs review

What: The supplied links use link.amazon/…, a format not described in the Associates Operating Agreement or Program Policies. Those documents define Special Links as links that “properly use the special ‘tagged’ link formats we provide,” with the tag appearing as XXXXX-## as a URL parameter.

What we are not saying: We do not claim the supplied links are invalid. Their exact compliance status is unresolved pending confirmation.

Why it matters: Only confirmed Special Links earn commission and satisfy the agreement; buyer CTAs currently use the documented /dp/…?tag= format instead.

Evidence: Operating Agreement §1 ↗ · Program Policies — Participation Requirements ↗

Suggested action: Confirm with Associates support; run the one-link test (S-02); then decide CTAs (S-01).

FLG-003Needs review

What: Intended ASIN, intended listing URL, confirmation date, and intent source are blank for all five rows of the product-intent record. Nothing public binds a 9-character short code to a product, so intent cannot be inferred from observation.

Why it matters: The record’s core rule is that intent and observation never merge; until the owner confirms, half the record stays empty on purpose.

Evidence: product-intent confirmation record

Suggested action: Owner fills intent columns (S-08).
