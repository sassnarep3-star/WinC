The three-part read

1 · What does “List Price” mean on this page?

Amazon attaches its own explanation to list prices. As displayed on our observed listings:

“The List Price is the suggested retail price of a new product as provided by a manufacturer, supplier, or seller… Amazon will display a List Price if the product has been purchased by customers on Amazon or offered by other retailers at or above the List Price in at least the past 90 days.”

Read that carefully: the reference price has a 90-day history rule behind it, and it’s a suggested price, not a promise of what anyone paid yesterday. It’s a useful anchor — just a labelled one.

2 · Is the badge describing a time-limited event?

“Black Friday Deal” badges on our pages sat next to countdown-style deal language. Event badges mean the price is expected to change when the event ends — which cuts both ways. If you need the item this season, the observed price is the fact; if you don’t, waiting is a legitimate strategy with no guarantee attached either direction. We don’t predict prices — we’d have no way to verify the prediction.

3 · Re-verify at click time

Our register prices were read on 20 Nov 2026. By the time you read this, they may differ. Every product link on this site goes to the official listing — check the number there before you add to cart. If our card and the listing disagree, the listing is the truth.

Deal checklist: (1) read what List Price means on the page, (2) note whether the badge is event-bound, (3) re-verify the live price at click, (4) ignore any claim — ours included — that can’t be tied to a dated source.
