Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

By Kitchen Shortlist Editorial· Updated 20 November 2026· 6 min read· Sources

Commissioned as an editorial hypothesis from the project brief's suggested topics — a working guide chosen for usefulness, not measured demand. See editorial policy.

"10-in-1" is not a feature list; it's a bet that at least three of those functions will see your counter. Multi-function appliances — toaster-oven hybrids, air fryer ovens, combo cookers — are wonderful buys when the questions below get answered before the purchase, and expensive shelf art when they don't.

Eight questions. Answer them in order.

The list

  1. Which three functions will I use weekly? Write them down. If you can't name three, you're buying a single-function appliance with extra buttons.
  2. Does each function work independently? Some combos share a timer or a mode — "air fry while toasting" may be sequential, not simultaneous.
  3. What actually fits inside? Multi-function claims lean on interior volume; your weekly cooking leans on rack positions and tray sizes. Check both in the listing.
  4. How many parts hit the sink per use? The cleanup guide applies double here: more functions usually means more accessories.
  5. What replaces what? If it replaces your toaster and your air fryer, count the counter space you get back. If it replaces nothing, it's adding, not consolidating.
  6. What's the failure surface? Doors, hinges, seals, fans and displays all wear. A simpler machine with fewer modes often outlives a gadget-rich one.
  7. Is it for me — or for someone I'm gifting? Gift buyers should optimize for the recipient's counter and cooking habits, not the feature count on the box.
  8. What does the return window look like? The only honest test of "will I use the rotisserie" is a month in a real kitchen.

For gift buyers

Ask the recipient three things anonymously: how wide is your free counter run, do you air-fry weekly already, and do you hand-wash or dishwasher everything? Gifted appliances fail on logistics, not on features. When in doubt, the shortlist's simpler machines make safer gifts than the 10-in-1.

The one-line version

Name your three weekly functions first — if the appliance can't beat what you already own at those three, no amount of extra modes will save it from the cabinet.
