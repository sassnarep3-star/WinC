A publication, not an auto-generated catalog

Curious by design.
Careful by default.

We want the site to read like an editor who respects your time: specific questions, plain-language trade-offs and evidence you can check.

Our evidence labels

  • Official product fact: copied accurately from a current brand or Amazon detail page, with a source and checked date.
  • Firsthand observation: based on a documented test; method, conditions and limitations are stated.
  • Editorial hypothesis: a proposed question or angle, not proof of demand, popularity, performance or superiority.
  • Unverified: left blank where authoritative confirmation is unavailable.

How a guide earns publication

  1. Pick a reader question and confirm the relevant product category.
  2. Build a source register; verify every factual claim against the cited source and retain a checked date.
  3. Separate manufacturer claims from independent observations. No review snippets, rating summaries or prices without authorized sourcing and current verification.
  4. Have a human editor check usefulness, factual accuracy, link destination, accessibility, sponsored attributes and disclosure visibility.
  5. Publish with a clear update date, canonical URL, sitemap entry and RSS item; correct material errors visibly.

What we won't imply

We will not claim to have tested a product unless a documented test took place. We will not say a product is best, trending, high-converting or popular without evidence. We will not invent commissions, sales, ratings, prices, ranking outcomes or reader demand.

Commercial disclosure and links

Amazon's Associates Agreement requires a clear, prominent statement identifying the site as an Associate. The FTC says affiliate relationships should be clearly and conspicuously disclosed, close to the recommendation where appropriate. We show the Amazon statement in the masthead strip and footer. Purchase links are held until the intended product, Special Link destination and report tracking have been tested.

References: Amazon Associates Operating Agreement · FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A.

Human sign-off gate

Automation drafts; an editor decides. Research generation, content drafting, sitemap/feed updates and monitoring can be automated with checks. Product identity, claims, testimonials, product comparisons, API policy questions, disclosures and final publication require accountable human approval. Automation must not bypass the gate.
