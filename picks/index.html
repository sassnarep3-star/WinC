Five links, zero confirmed products

About the address column. We print each supplied address instead of linking it: the destination is unconfirmed, the URL carries no tracking tag, and a reader should never be sent somewhere we cannot vouch for. Copy it to check it yourself, then confirm it with the linking requirements open.

Why the empty columns are empty. Title, listing URL, price, rating and redirect target all require opening a listing. Egress from the build environment to the store is refused (see FLG-003), and price and rating fields are additionally restricted by the program rules (FLG-005). We did not fill any of them in.

What a row here is for

  • a straight answer about whether we can cite the product at all
  • the exact identifier we were given, so the reader can check it independently
  • a link to the official store help page or listing when one exists
  • the decision criteria that do not depend on our data: capacity, footprint, cleaning, controls, and what to look for on the listing

The brief's guide topics concern air fryers and multi-function kitchen appliances. Whether the five links belong to that category is one of the unresolved items, so no guide names a product and no product row claims a category.

The one test worth running this week

Per the project brief: one link per product, in the enrolled Associates flow, confirming the destination and that tracking reports it — before scaling to all five. Two constraints on that test are worth stating out loud: a test purchase through your own link is prohibited, and purchases by friends, relatives or employees are too, so confirmation must come from the click showing up in the account's own reports, not from a bought item. And the short links as supplied are not declared invalid here; that question stays open.
