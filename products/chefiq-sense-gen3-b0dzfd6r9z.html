Three probes, no wire, and a phone that has to stay in range

The CHEF iQ Sense is a different category of object from a handheld probe: probes that stay in the food, a hub that talks to your phone, and a cook you monitor from another room.

Deciding spec: listed capacity — 3 probes + hub, as listed. Read it on Amazon

The listing describes three ultra-thin probes, a charging hub, WiFi range and a heat-safe claim up to 1000°F, with five sensors across the system and Gen3 in the name. The point of the design is the thing a handheld cannot do: leave the probe in the food, close the oven or smoker, and read the temperature without opening anything. For a long cook that is the difference between a roast you babysit and one you can leave.

The trade-off is a chain of dependencies that no single spec states. The probe must be in the food, the hub must be powered and within range of the probes, the phone must be connected to the hub, and the app must be running. Every link in that chain is a way for a cook to go unmonitored. USDA's position is blunt — a food thermometer is the only reliable way to confirm safety (S17) — so a wireless system that silently loses connection is worse than a cheap instant-read that never had one, unless you check the connection before you walk away.

Three probes is the spec that separates this listing from its own stablemate. Amazon's page shows a two-probe configuration of the same 'Sense' model family sold separately, with a different ASIN, and the price difference between them is not a difference in accuracy. If you are buying for a single roast, two probes is enough; the third earns its place when you are cooking more than one piece of meat at once, or one large piece where you want a second reading near a different part.

What the listing does not settle: the model string appears as a compact alphanumeric code, and the dimensions given on the page describe the package rather than the deployed hub. Neither is a reason to avoid the product; both are reasons to read the Amazon page's own specification block rather than a summary of it. The probes recharge through the hub, which is worth knowing because a probe that runs flat mid-cook is the failure mode this design invites — the listing says the hub charges them, not how long a charge lasts.

Who this is for: someone who already cooks with a phone nearby and wants a long cook monitored without opening the oven. Who it is not for: someone who wants to stab a chicken breast and read a number in one second — that is the instant-read job, and this system is slower to set up by design.

CHEF iQ's thermometers are wireless probes in a charging hub, paired with a phone app that runs the cook. Questions readers ask about this one

How many probes come with it?

The listing names 3 ultra-thin probes and a hub with 5 sensors, reporting over WiFi, with probes stated heat safe to 1000F. It also lists BBQ, grill, oven, smoker and air fryer among its uses.

Does it need an app?

The headline feature on the listing is WiFi reporting, so the reading arrives on a phone - which means the app, the hub and the router are part of the product. If you would rather not install an app, the instant-read in this shop is the other shape.

What does 'unlimited range' mean?

It is a marketing phrase on the listing. What it means is that the hub reports over your network rather than over a short radio link; how far the hub can sit from the cooker is a question about your router and your house, not a published number.
