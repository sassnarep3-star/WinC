26 quarts of cavity, and the two numbers on the listing that disagree

The Emeril 26QT is the only oven in this shop, and it is sized for a table rather than a plate. Its listing is also the most self-contradicting of the five.

Deciding spec: listed capacity — 26QT (24.6 Liters), as listed. Read it on Amazon

A 26-quart cavity is not a bigger air-fryer basket; it is a small oven with a fan. The listing describes French doors, a countertop convection cavity, and a long list of one-touch presets. The listed exterior is roughly 17 inches deep, 16 wide and 15 tall, and the unit is listed at 26.5 pounds. That is a machine that lives on a counter permanently, not one that comes out of a cupboard — and it needs headroom above it, because the doors open outward and the top gets hot.

The reason to choose it is capacity for a crowd: a cavity that takes a whole bird or a full tray of vegetables does a job no drawer fryer can. The listing's own bullet copy sizes the cavity for four to six people. That is a seller's claim about serving count, not a measurement, and this site quotes it as a claim.

Now the part that matters for anyone comparing on the spec sheet: the listing does not agree with itself, twice. The title says 24 one-touch preset options and six cooking accessories; the bullet copy gives 25 presets and seven accessories (FLG-010, FLG-020). Neither number changes how the appliance cooks. Both change how much you should trust a count on this listing — if you are buying specifically for a function you saw in the preset list, find that function named in the bullets before you decide.

There is also a genuine gap on this record. No maximum temperature and no wattage could be confirmed from the listing body in this session, so both cells are blank on the product page rather than inferred from a sibling model. For an oven-style unit the top of the temperature range is the figure that sets what it can do at the top of its range, so it is the first thing to establish on the Amazon page itself. The listing's serving claim — a cavity sized for four to six people, a 6-lb chicken and a 12-inch pizza — is the seller's own sizing statement, quoted here as a claim (FLG-021).

The decision this product asks is shape, not size. If your cooking is trays — a whole fish, a batch of fries and a tray of peppers at once — the cavity wins. If your cooking is two portions and a 25-minute limit, a 26-quart oven is a large object to preheat for a small job, and the drawer machines in this shop are the better fit.

What to compare before you buy

  • Capacity vs. your actual meals: 26QT (24.6 Liters) — measure the tray or bird you cook most often.
  • Footprint: 16.9"D x 15.8"W x 14.8"H — check counter depth and cabinet clearance.
  • Cleanup: check whether baskets/parts are listed as dishwasher-safe on Amazon before you buy.
  • Functions you will use weekly: presets only help if they match your routine (see the multi-function guide).

Questions readers ask about this one

Is it an air fryer or an oven?

The listing describes both: a 26-quart French-door air fryer toaster oven whose bullets name cooking modes including toast, ribs, slow cook and roast. Its details table lists 16.9"D x 15.8"W x 14.8"H and a capacity of 24.6 liters.

How many presets and accessories does it come with?

The listing disagrees with itself. The page title says 24 one-touch preset options and six cooking accessories, while the bullet heading says 25 cooking pre-sets, and an earlier capture of the same page read 7 accessories. Both readings are published on the Flagged for review page; this site picks neither (FLG-010, FLG-020).

How large a cook can it handle?

The listing's own bullet says the 26-quart cavity is sized for 4-6 people, can rotisserie a 6-lb chicken and bake a 12-inch pizza. That is the seller's sizing claim on the listing, quoted as a claim and not as advice from this desk (FLG-021).
