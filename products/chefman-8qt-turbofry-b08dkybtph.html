One basket, eight quarts, and a listing that argues with itself about heat

The 8-quart Chefman is the simplest machine in the shop and the one with the most muddled listing: its own title advertises a temperature its detail table does not support.

Deciding spec: listed temperature range — 200-400°F (listing also mentions 450°F Hi-Fry — see FLG-005), as listed. Read it on Amazon

This is the single-basket shape most people picture when they hear 'air fryer': one drawer, one nonstick basket, one temperature, one timer. At a listed eight quarts it is the largest single basket in this shop, and its listed weight — 12.8 pounds — is lighter than the 6-quart dual-basket machine, which is worth noticing if the appliance has to move between cooks.

The heat story is where the listing gets interesting. The title advertises a 450°F Hi-Fry option; the bullet copy states a 200–400°F range; the details table gives a different model string again. The honest reading is that the machine has a Hi-Fry setting the seller wants in the title, and a base range below it, and that the exact ceiling should be confirmed on the page you are sent to (FLG-005). This site does not resolve the conflict by picking the nicer number.

The other live irregularity is the wattage. An earlier capture in this repo recorded 1700 W for this model; the listing's bullet copy reads 1000 W at 120 V for the RJ38-SQSS-8T. The repo was corrected and the old figure kept beside it as provenance (FLG-022). It matters because 1000 W is a modest draw for an eight-quart cavity — it is the kind of machine that trades a little speed for a smaller electrical appetite, and a buyer with an old kitchen circuit may care about that more than they expect.

The ASIN itself is a family, not a single product. Amazon's own page offers the same model in 6-, 8- and 9-quart sizes under size variations, and states that a newer model of the item exists (FLG-023, FLG-024). Both facts are normal marketplace behaviour with one practical consequence: the review count and the price shown on the page belong to the variation you land on, so read the size selector before you read anything else.

Who this suits: a household that cooks one large thing at a time and wants the simplest possible control panel. Who it does not: anyone whose weeknight cooking is two components with different timings — that is the dual-basket case, and splitting an eight-quart batch to fake it wastes the capacity you paid for.

What to compare before you buy

  • Capacity vs. your actual meals: 8 Quarts — measure the tray or bird you cook most often.
  • Footprint: 12.75"D x 13.5"W x 10"H — check counter depth and cabinet clearance.
  • Cleanup: check whether baskets/parts are listed as dishwasher-safe on Amazon before you buy.
  • Functions you will use weekly: presets only help if they match your routine (see the multi-function guide).

Questions readers ask about this one

Which size will I actually get?

The ASIN is a size family: the page offers 6, 8 and 9 quarts and was showing 8 quarts selected on 2026-11-20, with the details table reading 'Capacity: 8 quarts'. Check the size selector on the page you land on, because the same link can carry a different size (FLG-023).

How much power does it draw?

The listing bullet reads '1000 Watts/120 Volts - RJ38-SQSS-8T'. An earlier capture of this page in this repo carried 1700 W, which is the wattage printed on the 6-quart Chefman listing; it was corrected on 2026-11-20 and the earlier value is kept beside the correction (FLG-022).

Does it really reach 450F?

The page title says '450F Hi-Fry Option' while the bullet copy on the same page says a temperature range from 200-400F. Both strings are shown on the specs table rather than one of them being quietly chosen (FLG-005).
