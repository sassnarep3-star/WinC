Air Fryers: basket or oven-style?

Three air fryers, two shapes. Compare capacity, footprint and cleanup on the specs you can check — then read the guides written for exactly those questions.

By the ShopNow Kitchen Desk · Updated 2026-11-20

Match to your situation

Each answer names the one listed spec that settles the case, and links the listing it came from. Nobody here has cooked with these units, so there is no “we tested it” line anywhere — you check the spec against your kitchen.

Two of you, most weeknights

Deciding spec: listed capacity

The 6-quart Chefman lists two 3-quart baskets. The 8-quart Chefman names one XL basket and an 8-quart capacity. The 26-quart Emeril oven-style unit lists a 26-quart cavity, and its own bullet copy says that cavity is sized for 4–6 people.

What that means for you: Start with one of the two Chefman fryers — the deciding spec is listed capacity, and the oven-style unit is sized by its own listing for a bigger table than yours.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CDY277D3 · listings read 2026-11-20.

Two dishes, ready at the same time

Deciding spec: what the listing says the product is

Only the 6-quart Chefman's listing names two independent baskets and a Sync Finish that lets them finish together. The 8-quart lists one 8-quart basket. The Emeril's two-way capability is rotisserie plus convection, not two baskets.

What that means for you: If finishing two foods at once is the goal, the dual-basket listing is the one that names the feature — check that the Sync Finish wording is on the page you are actually sent to.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CDY277D3 · listings read 2026-11-20.

You want oven functions, not just frying

Deciding spec: the listing's own claims

The Emeril listing describes a French-door toaster oven and names cooking modes including toast, ribs, slow cook, roast and keep warm; its bullets say 25 pre-sets while its title says 24. The two Chefman listings describe fryer, bake and dehydrate functions.

What that means for you: The oven-style listing is the only one of the three that names toaster-oven modes such as toast and rotisserie. Treat its preset count as unsettled until you see which number the page gives you.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09B7SB46R · listings read 2026-11-20.

Counter space is the constraint

Deciding spec: listed footprint

Listed footprints: 12.75"D × 13.5"W × 10"H for the 8-quart fryer, 13.38"D × 11.81"W × 10.63"H for the 6-quart dual basket, and 16.9"D × 15.8"W × 14.8"H for the 26-quart oven-style unit. Listed weights: 12.8 lb, 13.3 lb and 26.5 lb.

What that means for you: Measure depth before you measure anything else. The oven-style unit's listed depth is 4.15 inches deeper than the 8-quart fryer's, and it weighs more than twice as much to lift.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08DKYBTPH · listings read 2026-11-20.

You need a high-heat sear or bake

Deciding spec: listed temperature range

The 6-quart Chefman's bullet copy states a 200–450°F digital temperature range. The 8-quart Chefman's title says 450°F Hi-Fry while the same page's bullet copy says 200–400°F — the listing contradicts itself.

What that means for you: The deciding spec here is the maximum temperature, and only one of the three listings states a range without contradiction. Check the range on the product page, not on a comparison table.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CDY277D3 · listings read 2026-11-20.

You are buying for someone who cooks a lot

Deciding spec: the listing's own claims

Amazon's 8-quart Chefman page states there is a newer model of that item and shows a 6-quart Compact as the newer one. The same ASIN also offers 6, 8 and 9 quart size variants.

What that means for you: Before you pay for the 8-quart, open the listing and check the size selector and the 'newer model' note. Both are on the page, and both change what you are buying.

Source: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08DKYBTPH · listings read 2026-11-20.
